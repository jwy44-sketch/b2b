## Recurring Test Logic

- Prefer answers that preserve fair opportunity, adequate documentation, competition, clear requirements, market research, and documented rationale.
- Watch for absolute distractors such as "only after award," "only contracting personnel," "all risk eliminated," or "documentation is unnecessary."
- Separate actual authority from apparent authority; only properly authorized contracting officers can bind the Government.
- Treat market research as iterative and lifecycle-relevant, not a one-time pre-solicitation checkbox.
- Distinguish provisions from clauses: provisions generally apply before award; clauses apply after award or both solicitation and contract contexts when prescribed.
- For Part 15, focus on lifecycle logic: presolicitation, evaluation, exchanges, award, debriefing, and documentation.
- For price/cost questions, distinguish price analysis from cost analysis, certified cost or pricing data from other data, and fair and reasonable price from cost realism.
- For protests and disagreements, look for timely, documented, process-aware answers rather than informal shortcuts.
- For post-award questions, identify who has authority, what must be documented, and whether the issue is administration, change, quality, property, subcontracting, closeout, or termination.
