data.

FAR 15.103-2 Lowest price technically acceptable (LPTA) approach.
(a) The lowest price technically acceptable (LPTA) approach to source selection is appropriate when it is in the Government’s best interest to award to the 
offeror that submits a technically acceptable proposal with the lowest evaluated price. This approach does not permit tradeoffs. 
 (b) Required information – RFPs using LPTA must identify the evaluation factors that establish the minimum requirements for acceptability and specify 
that award will be made on the basis of the lowest evaluated price of the proposals meeting or exceeding the acceptability requirements.
FAR 15.103-1 Tradeoff approach.
The tradeoff approach to source selection is appropriate when it may be in the Government’s best interest to consider award to other than the lowest priced 
offeror or other than the highest technically rated offeror. This process permits tradeoffs among cost or price and non-cost or non-price factors. The 
anticipated benefits of a higher priced proposal must merit the additional cost.
FAR 15.103-3(a) Highest technically rated with a fair and reasonable price approach.
This approach is appropriate when determined in advance that it would not be advantageous to consider tradeoffs; rather, the acquisition warrants paying 
any fair and reasonable price for the highest quality performance. All proposals are evaluated based on the non-price factors in the solicitation and the 
source selection authority determines the highest technically rated proposal. The price of the highest technically rated proposal is then evaluated to 
determine if the price is fair and reasonable. 
FAR 15.103-4(a) Phased Approach. This approach breaks down complex or high-risk acquisitions into discrete parts, structured to make separate awards at 
distinct phases of the acquisition. This can reduce source selection timeframes and promote flexibility by permitting adjustments to the requirements 
between phases.
DoW Source Selection Procedures VATEP Tradeoff Process. Value Adjusted Total Evaluated Price Tradeoff
The VATEP process monetizes different levels of performance that may correspond to the traditional requirements process of defining both threshold 
(minimum) and objective (maximum) performance and capabilities. It identifies in the RFP the percentage price increase (or dollar amount) the Government 
is willing to pay for measureable levels of performance between threshold (minimum) and objective (maximum) criteria (e.g., Probability of Hit, specific 
operational ranges, etc.). This amount is based on the value to the Government for above minimum performance or capabilities.
