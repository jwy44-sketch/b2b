How the RFO Will Affect the Contracting (CON) Professional 
Certification Program – Student FAQ Sheet 
As of 27 Jan 2026 
Purpose of This FAQ Sheet 
This FAQ sheet is designed to help learners understand what the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul 
(RFO) means for the Contracting (CON) Professional Certification Program. It provides clear, up-
to-date answers on certification exam changes, course updates, timelines, and what to expect 
during the transition. Because the RFO is still being implemented across the Department of War 
(DOW), this is a living document and will be updated as additional information, updates, and 
timelines become available. The goal is to ensure learners receive one consistent message and 
can plan their certification path with confidence. 
 
CERTIFICATION EXAM 
1. When will the CON Certification Exam change due to the RFO? 
The exam will maintain 100% availability while the RFO updates are implemented 
through the deviation process. To the extent RFO updates drive changes to the current 
bank of exam questions, those changes will be incorporated all at once, and the 
workforce will be notified of the cutover schedule in the first quarter of CY2026. If any 
of those changes are subsequently affected by the rule-making process, then 
adjustments will be made accordingly, and an updated implementation schedule will be 
issued (if needed). 
 
2. How much will the certification exam change? 
RFO updates will affect approximately 30% of the current bank of exam questions. 
However, since the RFO updates do not have substantive impact on the DOW 
Contracting Competencies, those same core contracting competency-related questions 
on the exam are not expected to change significantly. The exam still relates directly to 
those competencies at the “initial readiness” level as defined in the DOW Contracting 
Competency Model. 
 
3. Did the recent threshold updates affect the certification exam? 
Because of how the questions are currently structured, the threshold changes do not 
impact the questions or answers. 

 
 
4. Should I wait to take the CON Professional Certification Exam until the RFO updates 
are incorporated? 
Students should consult with their supervisor and DACM/DATM office to determine the 
most appropriate timing for taking the exam. 
If you want to take the exam before the update 
You are encouraged to take the exam now if you have completed the certification 
courses and feel prepared. The fundamental competencies remain the same, so taking 
the exam before the RFO update is appropriate. 
If you want to wait until the update 
Students who prefer to wait should plan to take the updated CON 3910 once available, 
as it will help with familiarity of updated terminology and context. 
If you are currently enrolled in CON 1100/V – CON 1400/V 
Students may continue in their current certification courses or wait for the updated 
versions (see section below). Because the learning objectives are not changing 
significantly due to the RFO, either path remains viable. Students should consult with 
their supervisor and DACM/DATM office to determine whether to remain in their 
current course or switch to an updated version once available, and to determine the 
most appropriate timing for taking the exam. There is no need to retake any CON 
1100/V–CON 1400/V courses already completed. Regardless of the path chosen, 
students are encouraged to complete the updated CON 3910 before attempting the 
exam. 
 
CERTIFICATION COURSES 
1. When will certification courses (CON 1100/V–CON 1400/V) be updated with the RFO 
content? 
Certification courses are expected to be updated as soon as practicable after the DOW 
issues its class deviation(s) adopting the model deviation text. 
 
2. How much are the certification courses changing? 
The core learning objectives tied to “Initial Readiness” are not changing significantly. 
However, examples and terminology will be updated to reflect the RFO. 
 

 
3. When will prep courses CON 3910 and CON 3900V change due to the RFO? 
The prep courses will be updated as soon as practicable after the DOW issues its class 
deviation(s) adopting the model deviation text.
